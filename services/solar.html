Why Should Roofing and Solar Be Coordinated in Connecticut?

The most expensive solar mistake CT homeowners make is installing panels on a roof that needs replacement within the next 5–10 years. Solar panels carry 25-year production warranties. Asphalt shingle roofs in CT typically last 25–35 years depending on ventilation, installation quality, and weather exposure. If your roof is 15 years old when you install solar, there is a strong probability you will need a roof replacement — and a solar panel removal and reinstallation — within your system's warrantied lifespan.

The Real Cost of Removing and Reinstalling Solar Panels

Removing and reinstalling a standard 8–12 kW residential solar array for a roof replacement typically costs $3,000–$6,000 in labor alone, not including any roof patching required from old attachment points. This cost is almost never covered by the roofing contractor's warranty, the solar installer's warranty, or homeowner's insurance. It comes entirely out of pocket.

Our process: before recommending solar, we walk the roof and evaluate condition, estimated remaining life, and any structural or ventilation issues that should be addressed. If the roof has 15+ years of life remaining and is structurally sound, we proceed. If the roof needs replacement within 5–7 years, we recommend a coordinated roof-plus-solar project — one mobilization, one crew coordination, one contract. The incremental cost of adding a roof to a solar project is almost always less than a future disconnect-reroof-reconnect cycle.

Roof Structural Requirements for Solar in CT

Solar panel arrays add approximately 3–5 pounds per square foot of dead load to the roof structure. Most CT residential construction — stick-frame with standard rafter or truss systems — handles this load without modification. However, older homes (pre-1960) with board sheathing, unusually long rafter spans, or evidence of previous structural issues may need engineering review before installation. Our partnership team handles load analysis as part of the design process.

Penetration attachment and flashing are equally important. Every roof penetration for a panel mount creates a potential leak point. Our solar installations use lag bolt mounts with Dektite or similar flashing boots, sealed with compatible roofing sealant. GAF installation guidelines for solar penetrations are followed on all GAF-warranted roofs to preserve the roofing warranty.

What Solar Incentives Are Available in Connecticut?

Connecticut is one of the better states in the Northeast for solar economics, combining federal incentives, state net metering policy, and some of the highest residential electricity rates in the country. The following incentive programs were active as of our last update; incentive structures change and our solar partner provides current program details during the design phase.

Federal Investment Tax Credit (ITC)

The federal ITC provides a 30% tax credit on the total cost of a solar installation — including panels, inverters, racking, installation labor, and electrical work. This is a dollar-for-dollar reduction in federal income tax liability, not a deduction. For a $35,000 system, the ITC generates a $10,500 tax credit, reducing the effective out-of-pocket cost to $24,500. The credit can be carried forward if you cannot use the full amount in the installation year.

For commercial installations, the ITC applies at the same 30% rate, and commercial property owners can also take bonus depreciation on solar equipment under current tax law — often generating a combined year-one tax benefit of 50–60% of system cost. Commercial solar economics can be very compelling even in Connecticut's climate.

Connecticut Net Metering — Eversource and United Illuminating

Net metering allows CT solar customers to send excess electricity to the grid during high-production periods (midday, summer) and draw from the grid during low-production periods (evenings, winter) — with credits applied against their bill. Eversource and United Illuminating both offer net metering to qualifying customers under CT PURA regulations. The credit rate and billing mechanism are set by PURA and can change; our solar partner provides current net metering terms during design.

One important note specific to CT: the state has a relatively high avoided-cost rate for net metering compared to some other northeastern states, which improves solar economics. CT's high baseline electricity prices (consistently among the top 5 most expensive states) mean that each kWh your solar system produces is worth more in avoided cost than in states with lower rates.

CT Green Bank Financing

The Connecticut Green Bank offers below-market-rate financing for solar installations for qualifying CT homeowners, including zero-down options. This allows homeowners to go solar with no upfront cost and pay for the system through savings on their electric bill. Our solar partner handles all Green Bank documentation and financing coordination for eligible customers.

Commercial: Bonus Depreciation

Commercial property owners can combine the 30% ITC with bonus depreciation on the remaining 70% of system cost. Under current federal tax law, this generates a very significant year-one tax benefit. A $200,000 commercial solar installation with full bonus depreciation and the ITC can generate first-year tax benefits exceeding $100,000 for a business in a high marginal tax bracket. This dramatically compresses the effective payback period for commercial installations.

How Much Does Solar Cost in Connecticut?

Solar system costs vary based on system size (kW), panel brand and efficiency, inverter type, roof complexity, and electrical service requirements. The following ranges reflect typical installed costs in Connecticut before incentives.

Residential Solar System Costs in CT

  • Small system (6–8 kW): $20,000–$30,000 installed before incentives. After 30% ITC: $14,000–$21,000 effective cost.
  • Average CT home (8–12 kW): $25,000–$45,000 installed. After 30% ITC: $17,500–$31,500.
  • Larger home / high usage (12–16 kW): $40,000–$60,000. After ITC: $28,000–$42,000.

Payback period in Connecticut typically runs 7–10 years for residential systems, accounting for the ITC and CT electricity rates. After payback, the remaining 15+ years of system life represents near-free electricity production. Over a 25-year ownership period, a properly sized CT solar system typically generates $50,000–$90,000 in electricity value.

Commercial Solar System Costs in CT

Commercial systems are priced on a per-watt basis and generally benefit from economies of scale on larger installations. Typical installed cost range is $2.50–$3.50 per watt before incentives. A 100 kW commercial rooftop system runs $250,000–$350,000 installed, with effective cost after ITC and bonus depreciation often falling to $100,000–$140,000. Payback periods for commercial installations with full tax benefits typically run 5–8 years.

Battery Storage Add-On

Battery storage (Tesla Powerwall and similar systems) can be added to residential solar installations. Batteries allow homeowners to store excess midday production for evening use and provide backup power during grid outages. Battery systems add $10,000–$20,000 to system cost depending on capacity. The ITC applies to battery systems installed alongside solar. For CT homeowners concerned about power outages — increasingly relevant given CT's storm exposure — battery backup is worth evaluating as part of the initial design.

Solar Site Assessment: What We Evaluate

A solar site assessment for a CT property covers: roof condition and remaining life (see above), roof orientation and pitch (south-facing roofs at 30–40 degrees are optimal; east/west-facing roofs at lower pitch still work well), shading analysis from trees and neighboring structures, current electric usage and bill review, utility interconnection requirements (Eversource vs. United Illuminating service area), and available roof area for the target system size. Frequently Asked Questions — Connecticut Solar

Does Energy Efficient Roofer install residential solar?

Yes, through our partnership with SmartRoof Solar / Solar Homes 360. We handle both roof assessment/replacement and solar installation under a coordinated project, so you never have to worry about whether your roof can support your panels. Our approach is roofing-first: we assess the roof before any solar design work begins, address any issues, and then size and install the solar system on a roof that is guaranteed to outlast the panels.

Will solar panels void my GAF roofing warranty?

Solar panels installed through our partnership follow GAF installation guidelines for penetrations and attachment. We use proper lag bolt mounts with code-compliant flashing boots, sealed with GAF-compatible sealants. Improperly installed solar penetrations — including lag bolts without flashing, or sealant that is not compatible with the roofing membrane — can and do void GAF manufacturer warranties. This is exactly why coordinating roofing and solar installation with the same contractor matters. When we install both the roof and the solar, there is no question about warranty compliance.

Is Connecticut a good state for solar?

Yes. CT receives 4.5–5 peak sun hours daily on average, which is sufficient for economic solar production. This is less than the Southwest but comparable to Germany, which installed solar at scale long before many other markets. Combined with CT's high electricity rates — consistently among the top 5 in the US at $0.25–$0.30+ per kWh — and the available federal ITC, solar ROI in CT is typically better than many sunnier states with lower electricity costs. The economics are driven more by what you pay for electricity than by how much sun you get.

What is the CT solar rebate program?

Connecticut's primary solar incentives include the federal 30% Investment Tax Credit (ITC) — a dollar-for-dollar tax credit on the full system cost — net metering through Eversource or United Illuminating that credits excess production against your bill, and zero-interest Green Bank loans for qualifying homeowners. The CT Green Bank also administers commercial solar financing programs. Our solar partner handles all incentive documentation, interconnection applications, and Green Bank paperwork as part of the project.
