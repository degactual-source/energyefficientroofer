What Commercial Roofing Systems Does EER Install in Connecticut?

The right commercial roofing system depends on the building's use, roof geometry, existing substrate condition, drainage design, and budget. Connecticut's climate — heavy snowfall, freeze-thaw cycling, high summer humidity, and severe storm events — imposes specific performance demands on commercial roofing membranes. Here is how each major system performs in CT conditions.

TPO (Thermoplastic Polyolefin) — Our Most Recommended CT Commercial System

TPO is a single-ply membrane installed in widths of 10, 12, or 20 feet, with seams heat-welded using hot-air tools. Heat-welded seams are a critical advantage in CT: unlike adhesive or taped seams, welded TPO seams become stronger than the membrane itself once properly fused. They resist freeze-thaw cycling, thermal expansion and contraction, and do not delaminate over time the way adhesive-bonded seams can.

Modern TPO is available in 60-mil and 80-mil thickness. We specify 80-mil TPO as our standard for new installations — the additional thickness provides better puncture resistance and improved durability on roofs with rooftop HVAC, regular maintenance traffic, or exposure to heavy debris. 60-mil is appropriate for recover applications over existing substrates. White TPO reflects solar radiation, reducing cooling loads in summer — relevant for commercial building energy costs in CT.

TPO systems include fully adhered, mechanically fastened, and ballasted installation methods. The appropriate method depends on wind uplift requirements (ASCE 7 wind exposure category for the specific site), deck substrate, and whether the installation is a new build or a recover. Most CT commercial TPO replacements use mechanical attachment to plywood, steel deck, or concrete deck substrates.

EPDM (Ethylene Propylene Diene Monomer) — Proven Performance in CT Winters

EPDM is a synthetic rubber membrane with a track record dating to the 1960s. It remains one of the most durable and weather-resistant commercial roofing options available, with demonstrated 30+ year service life on well-maintained installations. Black EPDM absorbs heat, which helps with winter snow melt but increases cooling loads in summer. White-coated EPDM is available for energy-conscious applications.

EPDM's primary advantage in CT commercial applications is flexibility. Unlike TPO, EPDM can be cold-applied — glued with bonding adhesive rather than welded — making it easier to install around complex penetration fields (HVAC curbs, pipes, conduits, skylights). Buildings with heavy rooftop equipment are often better served by EPDM where a welding machine cannot easily navigate between obstacles. Seam tape systems for EPDM have improved significantly; self-adhering EPDM seam tape now provides reliable performance in freeze-thaw conditions.

Modified Bitumen (Mod-Bit) — Torch-Down and Cold-Applied

Modified bitumen is an asphalt-based system reinforced with polyester or fiberglass and modified with APP (atactic polypropylene) or SBS (styrene-butadiene-styrene) polymer to improve flexibility at low temperatures. Mod-bit is available in torch-applied (heat-fused to substrate) and cold-applied (adhered with cold-process adhesive) formats. SBS mod-bit is preferred in CT for its superior cold-temperature flexibility — it remains pliable at temperatures where APP can crack.

Mod-bit is commonly used for low-slope applications and as a cap sheet over built-up roofing systems. It handles complex geometry and penetration-heavy roofs well, and provides good redundancy with multi-ply assemblies. We use mod-bit primarily on recovery projects over existing BUR systems and on buildings where the penetration density makes single-ply membrane impractical.

Standing Seam Metal — Long-Term Performance for Sloped Commercial Roofs

Standing seam metal is appropriate for commercial roofs with a pitch of 2:12 or greater. It is the correct long-term answer for schools, churches, institutional buildings, and higher-end commercial construction where a 40–50 year service life is the goal. Concealed fastener standing seam panels lock together at raised seams, eliminating exposed fastener penetrations entirely. Thermal expansion is managed through floating clip attachment. Steel with Kynar 500 finish or Galvalume substrate provides excellent corrosion resistance in CT's humid coastal climate.

Built-Up Roofing (BUR) — Legacy Systems

Built-up roofing — multiple layers of bitumen and reinforcing ply sheets, topped with aggregate surfacing — is the original flat roof system with a 100+ year track record. New BUR installations are rare today, but EER handles BUR repairs and evaluates BUR systems for recover vs. full replacement decisions. Many older CT commercial buildings carry 3-ply or 4-ply BUR systems that still have useful life if properly maintained. When a BUR system does fail, it is typically recovered with TPO or EPDM rather than replaced in-kind.

How Much Does Commercial Roof Replacement Cost in Connecticut?

Commercial roofing cost is typically quoted per square foot of roof area, including membrane, insulation, fasteners, flashings, and installation labor. The following ranges reflect typical installed costs for CT commercial projects as of recent years; actual quotes vary by site complexity, access conditions, disposal costs, and membrane specification.

Cost Ranges by System Type

  • TPO single-ply with insulation, mechanically fastened: $7–$14 per sq ft installed. Wide range reflects insulation R-value specification (code minimum vs. enhanced), membrane thickness (60 vs. 80 mil), and roof complexity.
  • EPDM single-ply with insulation, fully adhered: $6–$12 per sq ft installed.
  • Modified bitumen (2-ply SBS system): $8–$13 per sq ft installed.
  • Standing seam metal (Galvalume or painted steel): $15–$25 per sq ft installed.
  • TPO recover over existing substrate (no tear-off): $5–$9 per sq ft — significantly less when tear-off is eliminated.

Total Project Cost Estimates

  • 5,000 sq ft commercial roof: $35,000–$70,000 depending on system.
  • 10,000 sq ft commercial roof: $70,000–$140,000.
  • 20,000 sq ft commercial roof: $140,000–$280,000.

Insulation is often the largest single cost variable. CT energy code requires minimum R-values for commercial roofing assemblies; older buildings may be significantly under-insulated, and a roof replacement is the best — and sometimes only — opportunity to bring insulation to current standards. The additional insulation cost is frequently offset by energy savings within 5–10 years.

What Are the Signs a Commercial Roof in CT Needs Replacement?

Commercial roof systems tend to fail gradually rather than all at once. The decision to repair vs. recover vs. fully replace depends on the membrane's age, the extent and pattern of failures, the condition of the underlying insulation, and the cost trajectory of ongoing repairs. Here are the key indicators we look for during a commercial roof assessment.

Ponding Water

Water that ponds on a flat commercial roof for 48+ hours after rain indicates inadequate slope or blocked drainage. Standing water adds structural load (approximately 5 lbs per sq ft per inch of depth), degrades membrane adhesion over time, and accelerates biological growth. Commercial roofs are designed to drain within 48 hours; chronic ponding means either the roof was not installed to proper slope (minimum 1/8" per foot for commercial low-slope roofing) or the drains are undersized or blocked. Ponding water that cannot be addressed by drain clearing or adding internal drains is a replacement indicator.

Membrane Blistering, Splitting, or Delamination

Blisters in TPO or EPDM form when moisture is trapped between the membrane and the insulation. Heat causes the moisture to vaporize and pressure the membrane from below. Small isolated blisters can be repaired; a roof with widespread blistering has a systemic moisture intrusion problem in the insulation — and once the insulation is saturated, recovery is often not viable without tear-off. Splitting along seams or at flashings, and EPDM delamination at taped seams, indicate end-of-life for those sections.

Repeated Internal Leaks in the Same Location

A roof that has been repaired at the same location more than twice within a few years is communicating that the underlying problem has not been solved. Water infiltration in commercial buildings typically travels laterally through the insulation before finding an exit point in the interior — so the ceiling stain may be 20 feet from the actual membrane failure. If you are repeatedly calling for service at the same location, a full thermal scan to locate moisture in the insulation is the right next step.

Insulation Moisture Saturation

Wet insulation in a commercial roof assembly is invisible from above but detectable with thermal infrared scanning. During a clear night after a warm day, wet insulation retains heat longer than dry insulation — creating detectable thermal signatures on a properly conducted scan. We recommend thermal scanning for any commercial roof over 15 years old before committing to a recover vs. replace decision. Recovering wet insulation does not dry it out; it seals the moisture in and accelerates deck corrosion.

System Age

Age alone does not dictate replacement, but it does change the calculus. TPO and EPDM systems are typically designed for 20–25 year service life; at 15+ years, the system is entering its final third of expected life. Repair costs on aging systems tend to escalate. A 15-year-old commercial roof with minor issues may be better served by a full recover than another cycle of patching. We provide an honest assessment of where the system is in its life cycle and what the expected cost trajectory looks like under both scenarios.

What Qualifies as a Covered Loss on a CT Commercial Roof

Commercial property policies typically cover sudden, accidental physical damage from covered perils including: hail (which physically damages TPO, EPDM, and metal membranes above a certain impact threshold), wind (which can lift membrane edges, damage flashings, and tear off entire sections), falling objects (trees, ice), and in some cases ice dam backup damage. Maintenance issues, wear and tear, and gradual deterioration are generally excluded — which is why thorough damage documentation by a qualified roofing contractor is critical to a successful commercial claim.

Our Commercial Claims Process

We conduct a thorough physical inspection including roof surface, flashings, penetrations, gutters, copings, and building envelope components. We photograph all damage with measurement references. For suspected hail claims, we map impact patterns and measure indent sizes. We provide the property manager and owner with a written damage report before the carrier's adjuster walks the roof, so the scope is clearly established. If the carrier's initial estimate is inadequate — missing line items, undervaluing materials, or ignoring code upgrade requirements — we supplement the estimate with detailed documentation and current cost data.

Commercial roofing claims over $100,000 often involve appraisal processes, engineering reviews, and extended negotiations. Our experience with $2M+ in total managed claims means we know what to expect from the carrier, how to respond, and what documentation holds up through the process.

Frequently Asked Questions — Commercial Roofing in CT

What is the best commercial roofing system for Connecticut weather?

TPO is our most recommended system for CT commercial buildings. The heat-welded seams outperform adhesive seams in freeze-thaw cycles — a key advantage in a state where rooftop temperatures swing from -10°F to 150°F over the course of a year. The white membrane reduces cooling loads in summer, and modern 80-mil TPO handles the mechanical demands of CT winters reliably. EPDM is excellent for buildings with complex penetration layouts where a welding machine cannot easily navigate, and for buildings where the added flexibility in very cold temperatures is a priority. We specify TPO as our default and use EPDM on a case-by-case basis where it is genuinely the better fit.

How long does a commercial roof replacement take in Connecticut?

A 10,000–15,000 sq ft commercial roof typically takes 5–10 working days for full tear-off and replacement. Recover projects (installing new membrane over existing substrate without tear-off) are faster, often 3–5 days for the same area. Work is sequenced and phased to keep the interior protected at all times — no section of roof is left open to weather overnight. We coordinate with property managers on access scheduling, equipment staging, dumpster placement, and any tenant or operations impact. Larger projects (20,000+ sq ft) are planned in phases that may extend over 2–3 weeks.

Does EER provide preventive maintenance contracts for commercial roofs in CT?

Yes. Annual maintenance programs include spring and fall inspections, minor repairs (lap sealant, flashing touch-up, drain clearing), and written condition reports. Preventive maintenance extends roof life 5–10 years by catching minor issues before they become major ones. It also provides a documented maintenance record — which carriers use when evaluating whether a loss is storm damage vs. deferred maintenance. Property managers who can show a regular maintenance history have a stronger position in storm damage claims than those who cannot. We offer single-year and multi-year maintenance contracts for commercial customers.

What is the warranty on a new commercial TPO roof?

Standard manufacturer warranties on TPO cover materials for 15–20 years depending on the manufacturer and specification. GAF EverGuard TPO is available with 20-year warranties. EER's workmanship warranty covers installation defects for 5 years. On larger commercial projects (typically $50,000+), manufacturer NDL (No Dollar Limit) warranties are available — these cover both materials and labor costs for the warranty period, including tear-off and replacement if the membrane fails. NDL warranties require a manufacturer-registered installer and a final inspection; we coordinate the inspection and registration as part of the project closeout.
