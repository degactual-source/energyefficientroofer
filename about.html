What is GAF Certified Plus and why does it matter?

GAF Certified Plus is an independent contractor certification issued by GAF, North America's largest roofing manufacturer. To earn and maintain it, a contractor must carry proper state licensing, maintain current liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage, and demonstrate adherence to installation standards. It's not a designation you purchase or earn by meeting a sales quota — it's verification that the basics are in order and that the contractor is accountable to a third party.

You'll see other roofing companies in Connecticut advertise "Master Elite" status — GAF's highest-tier certification. EER doesn't claim it. Not because it's out of reach, but because honesty matters more than a marketing upgrade. EER is GAF Certified Plus. That's what it is. Misrepresenting a certification level — even a small upgrade — tells you something about how a contractor operates. Mike's view: if they'll shade the truth on a credential, what else are they shading?

What the GAF Certified Plus certification means practically: when EER installs a GAF roofing system, it qualifies for GAF's enhanced warranty coverage. That means more protection for the homeowner, backed by the manufacturer — not just the contractor's word. In a climate like Connecticut's, with freeze-thaw cycles, nor'easters, and occasional hurricane remnants, having that manufacturer-backed warranty is real insurance, not just a certificate on the wall.

Why does EER specialize in insurance claims?

It started the way most things do in the trades — a neighbor called. After a bad storm rolled through Fairfield County, a homeowner Mike knew asked him to take a look at the damage before the adjuster came out. Mike walked the roof, documented what the storm had done, and sat in on the adjuster's inspection. He saw line items excluded that clearly should have been included. He knew how to read the estimate and knew what a proper restoration scope looked like.

That homeowner got a fair settlement. Word spread. Now Mike is called in regularly — not just to do the roofing work, but to represent the homeowner's interests from the first inspection through the final check. EER currently manages over $2 million in active storm damage claims across Connecticut and New York.

The insurance claim process for roofing is not straightforward. Adjusters work for the insurance company. Their job is to scope the damage accurately, but they're also working under guidelines that incentivize lower payouts. A contractor who knows what a complete scope looks like — including flash, starter, drip edge, ventilation, and code upgrade requirements that Connecticut mandates on a full replacement — can submit a supplement that brings the settlement to where it should be. Mike does this regularly and successfully.

EER doesn't charge separately for claim management on jobs where EER is doing the work. The inspection is free. The documentation is done as part of the job. The supplementing is handled by Mike directly, not outsourced to a public adjuster you've never met. If you've had storm damage and haven't filed yet, or if you've filed and feel like the settlement offer doesn't cover what needs to be done, a call to Mike is worth your time.
